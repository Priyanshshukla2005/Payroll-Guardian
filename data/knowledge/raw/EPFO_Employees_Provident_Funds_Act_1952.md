## Section 6: Contributions and Matters Which May Be Provided For in Schemes

### 1. Mandatory Statutory Contribution Rates
The contribution which shall be paid by the employer to the Provident Fund shall be **12% of the basic wages, dearness allowance, and retaining allowance** (if any) for the time being payable to each of the employees (whether employed directly by such employer or by or through a contractor).

The employee's contribution shall be equal to the contribution payable by the employer in respect of him:
- **Employee Contribution**: 12.0% of Basic Salary + DA
- **Employer Contribution**: 12.0% of Basic Salary + DA

### 2. Breakdown of Employer's Statutory Contribution
The employer's 12% contribution is split as follows:
- **3.67%** credited directly to the employee's **Employees' Provident Fund (EPF) Account**.
- **8.33%** credited to the **Employees' Pension Scheme (EPS)**, subject to the statutory wage ceiling.

### 3. Statutory Wage Ceiling Threshold
The statutory wage ceiling for mandatory EPF coverage is **₹15,000 per month**. 
- For employees earning a basic wage up to ₹15,000 per month, contribution at 12% is mandatory.
- For employees earning basic wages exceeding ₹15,000 per month, the employer and employee may mutually agree to contribute either on the statutory ceiling of ₹15,000 (i.e. ₹1,800/month) or on the actual higher basic salary.
- In modern enterprise software practice, standard deduction is calculated as `0.12 * basic_salary`. Any deviation exceeding rounding tolerances constitutes a statutory calculation error.

### 4. Administrative and EDLI Charges
In addition to the 12% contribution:
- **EPF Administrative Charges (Account 2)**: 0.50% of basic wages.
- **Employees' Deposit Linked Insurance (EDLI) Contribution (Account 21)**: 0.50% of basic wages (capped at ₹15,000 wage ceiling = max ₹75/month).

### 5. Penalty for Non-Compliance and Default
Under Section 14 of the Act, failure to deposit provident fund contributions within 15 days of the close of the month attracts statutory damages under Section 14B ranging from 5% to 25% per annum alongside penal interest under Section 7Q at 12% per annum.
